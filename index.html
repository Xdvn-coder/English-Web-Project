Grammar Tense

A tense is a verb form that indicates when an event happens. In English, there are 16 tenses divided by time (past, present, future) and aspect (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous).

⏱ Present Tenses

TenseFormulaExample
Simple PresentS + V1 (s/es)She reads every day.
Present ContinuousS + is/am/are + V-ingThey are studying now.
Present PerfectS + has/have + V3I have finished my homework.
Present Perfect ContinuousS + has/have been + V-ingHe has been working for 3 hours.
💡

Simple Present is used for habits, general truths, and schedules. Keywords: always, usually, often, every day.

🕰 Past Tenses

TenseRumusContoh
Simple PastS + V2She visited Bali last year.
Past ContinuousS + was/were + V-ingWe were eating when he called.
Past PerfectS + had + V3She had left before I arrived.
Past Perfect ContinuousS + had been + V-ingHe had been waiting for hours.
💡

Past Perfect is used to show an event completed before another past event. Keywords: before, after, when, already, by the time.

🔮 Future Tenses

TenseRumusContoh
Simple FutureS + will + V1I will call you tomorrow.
Future ContinuousS + will be + V-ingShe will be studying at 7 PM.
Future PerfectS + will have + V3They will have finished by noon.
Future Perfect ContinuousS + will have been + V-ingHe will have been working for 5 years.

🎯 Tips Memilih Tense yang Tepat

  • Perhatikan kata kunci waktu seperti: yesterday, ago, last → Simple Past
  • Kata kunci: now, at this moment, currently → Present Continuous
  • Kata kunci: since, for → Present/Past Perfect atau Perfect Continuous
  • Kata kunci: tomorrow, next week, in the future → Future tenses
  • Dua kejadian di masa lalu → kejadian lebih dulu pakai Past Perfect, yang belakangan pakai Simple Past

✅ When I arrived (Simple Past), she had already left (Past Perfect).

✅ I have been living here since 2010. (Present Perfect Continuous)

Conditional Tense

Conditional sentences are used to talk about situations that may or may not happen. There are 5 types of conditionals in English.

📗 Type 0 — Zero Conditional

Use: General truths, natural laws, something always true.

Rumus: If + Simple Present, Simple Present

✅ If you heat water to 100°C, it boils.

✅ If it rains, the ground gets wet.

📘 Type 1 — First Conditional

Use: A condition that may happen in the future (real condition).

Rumus: If + Simple Present, will + V1

✅ If it rains tomorrow, I will stay at home.

✅ If she studies hard, she will pass the exam.

💡

You can use unless instead of "if not": Unless you hurry, you will miss the bus.

📙 Type 2 — Second Conditional

Use: An unreal or hypothetical condition in the present or future.

Rumus: If + Simple Past, would/could/might + V1

✅ If I were rich, I would travel the world.

✅ If she knew the answer, she would tell us.

⚠️

In Second Conditional, subjects I/he/she/it use were (not was) in formal English.

📕 Type 3 — Third Conditional

Use: An unreal past condition (regret or past hypothetical).

Rumus: If + Past Perfect, would/could/might + have + V3

✅ If I had studied harder, I would have passed.

✅ If she had taken the medicine, she would have recovered.

🔀 Mixed Conditional

Use: Combines a past condition with a present result, or the other way around.

Rumus A (Past → Present): If + Past Perfect, would + V1

Rumus B (Present → Past): If + Simple Past, would + have + V3

✅ If I had studied medicine (past), I would be a doctor now (present).

✅ If she were more careful (present), she wouldn't have made that mistake (past).

📊 Comparison Summary

TypeIf-clauseMain ClauseMakna

Type 1Simple Presentwill + V1Possible future
Type 2Simple Pastwould + V1Unreal present
Type 3Past Perfectwould have + V3Unreal past

Error Recognition

Error Recognition is the skill of finding and identifying grammar mistakes in a sentence. Questions usually show an underlined part, and you must choose the incorrect section.

🔴 Tipe Error 1: Wrong Verb Form

Errors in choosing the correct verb form (V1, V2, V3, V-ing).

❌ She has went to the market. → ✅ She has gone to the market.

❌ He was study when I called. → ✅ He was studying when I called.

🔴 Tipe Error 2: Wrong Tense

Using the wrong tense for the time context in the sentence.

❌ I have seen her yesterday. → ✅ I saw her yesterday.

❌ She was studying when the phone had rung. → ✅ ...the phone rang.

🔴 Tipe Error 3: Wrong Pronoun

Incorrect pronoun choice (subject, object, possessive).

❌ Between you and I, this is wrong. → ✅ Between you and me (object).

❌ Him and I went to school. → ✅ He and I went to school (subject).

🔴 Tipe Error 4: Wrong Article (a/an/the)

Incorrect or inappropriate use of articles (a/an/the).

❌ She is an university student. → ✅ She is a university student ("u" bersuara /j/).

❌ He plays the football every day. → ✅ He plays — football (olahraga tidak pakai "the").

🔴 Tipe Error 5: Wrong Preposition

Incorrect preposition use after a verb, adjective, or certain nouns.

❌ She is good in English. → ✅ She is good at English.

❌ He is interested about music. → ✅ He is interested in music.

❌ I arrived to Jakarta at noon. → ✅ I arrived in Jakarta at noon.

🔴 Tipe Error 6: Double Negative & Redundancy

Sentences with double negatives or unnecessary redundancy.

❌ I don't know nothing. → ✅ I don't know anything.

❌ She returned back home. → ✅ She returned home ("return" already means to go back).

❌ He is more cleverer than me. → ✅ He is cleverer than me.

🎯 Error Recognition Strategy

  • Read the whole sentence first to understand the context
  • Check each underlined part one by one
  • Check: is the verb form correct? Is the tense right?
  • Check: are the article, preposition, and pronoun correct?
  • Avoid getting trapped by choices that "sound" right but are grammatically wrong
  • If unsure, eliminate the clearly correct options

Relative Pronoun

Relative Pronouns are used to connect a relative clause to the noun it describes. A relative clause gives extra information about that noun.

👤 WHO — for a person (as subject)

Used for: Replacing a person as the subject.

✅ The teacher who taught us English was very kind.

✅ The girl who sits next to me is my best friend.

💡 "Who" replaces: he/she/they (as subject)

👤 WHOM — for a person (as object)

Used for: Replacing a person as the object (more formal).

✅ The man whom I met yesterday is a doctor.

✅ She is the person to whom I wrote the letter.

💡 "Whom" replaces: him/her/them (as object)

💡

Trick: If you can replace it with "him/her" → use whom. If you can replace it with "he/she" → use who.

🏠 WHOSE — possession

Used for: Showing possession (person or thing).

✅ The student whose book was stolen cried.

✅ I know a man whose wife is a famous singer.

✅ The house whose roof is red belongs to my uncle.

💡 "Whose" = his/her/their/its

🐾 WHICH — for things/animals

Used for: Replacing non-human nouns (things, animals, ideas).

✅ The book which I bought yesterday is interesting.

✅ The car which broke down has been repaired.

✅ This is the movie which won many awards.

🎯 THAT — for a person or thing

Used for: Replacing who or which in a defining relative clause (no commas).

✅ The man that I saw was wearing a red jacket.

✅ The book that she gave me was amazing.

✅ The only thing that matters is your happiness.

⚠️

That CANNOT be used in a non-defining relative clause (sentence with commas) or after a preposition.

📊 Relative Pronoun Comparison Table

PronounAntecedentFunctionExample
WhoPersonSubjectThe man who called
WhomPersonObjectThe man whom I met
WhosePerson/ThingPossessionThe girl whose bag
WhichThing/AnimalSubject/ObjectThe car which crashed
ThatPerson/ThingSubject/ObjectThe book that I read

🔵 Defining vs Non-Defining Relative Clause

  • Defining (no commas): Essential information to identify the noun. Can use that/who/which.
  • Non-defining (with commas): Extra information that can be removed. CANNOT use "that".

✅ Defining: The student who studies hard will succeed.

✅ Non-defining: My brother, who lives in Jakarta, is a doctor.

Subject-Verb Agreement

Subject-Verb Agreement is the rule for matching the subject with the verb in a sentence. Singular subject → singular verb, plural subject → plural verb.

📌 Basic Rule

A singular subject uses a singular verb (+ s/es), a plural subject uses a plural verb (no s/es).

✅ She reads books every night. (singular)

✅ They read books every night. (plural)

✅ The dog barks loudly. (singular)

✅ The dogs bark loudly. (plural)

📌 Rule 2: Compound Subject (and)

Two subjects joined by "and" = plural verb.

✅ Tom and Jerry are best friends.

✅ Rice and bread are staple foods.

⚠️ Exception: if two subjects refer to the same thing → singular.

✅ Bread and butter is my favorite breakfast. (satu konsep)

📌 Rule 3: Or / Nor / Either...or / Neither...nor

The verb agrees with the nearest subject.

✅ Either she or her friends are responsible. (friends = plural)

✅ Neither the students nor the teacher was there. (teacher = singular)

✅ John or his brothers have the key. (brothers = plural)

📌 Rule 4: Collective Nouns

Collective nouns (team, group, family, committee) can be singular or plural depending on context.

✅ The team is playing well. (sebagai satu unit)

✅ The team are arguing among themselves. (individu dalam tim)

📌 Rule 5: Indefinite Pronouns

  • Always singular: everyone, everybody, everything, someone, somebody, anyone, nobody, each, either, neither, one
  • Always plural: both, many, few, several, others
  • Can be singular/plural: all, some, none, most, any (depends on the following noun)

✅ Everyone is invited to the party.

✅ Both of them are doctors.

✅ Some of the water is gone. (singular noun)

✅ Some of the students are absent. (plural noun)

📌 Rule 6: Phrases Between Subject and Verb

The verb must agree with the main subject, NOT with the noun in the phrase between subject and verb.

✅ The box of chocolates is on the table. (The box = singular)

✅ The students in my class are smart. (The students = plural)

❌ The box of chocolates are on the table. ❌

📌 Rule 7: Numbers & Quantities

  • Phrases expressing amounts of money, time, distance → singular verb
  • The number of + plural noun → singular verb
  • A number of + plural noun → plural verb

✅ Ten dollars is not enough. (uang = satu jumlah)

✅ The number of students is increasing.

✅ A number of students are absent today.

📌 Additional Important Rules

  • Words ending in -s but with singular meaning: economics, mathematics, news, physics → singular
  • Gerund as subject → singular: Swimming is good for health.
  • Relative clauses don't affect the main subject-verb agreement
  • There is/are: agree with the following noun

✅ Mathematics is my favorite subject.

✅ There are many books on the shelf.

✅ There is a book on the desk.
